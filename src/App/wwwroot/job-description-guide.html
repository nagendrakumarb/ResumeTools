Professional Hub / Job-description guide

Read job descriptions for evidence, not just keywords

A job description is a summary of what an employer hopes the successful applicant can do. It often mixes essential duties, preferred qualifications, company information, benefits, legal wording, and generic language. Treating every word as a resume keyword produces a less accurate application.

Separate the important requirements

Read the description once, then sort its statements into four groups: core responsibilities, required experience, useful tools or methods, and preferences. Core responsibilities describe the work the person will actually perform. Required experience tells you the baseline the employer expects. Tools and methods may be worth naming when you have used them. Preferences can help differentiate applicants but should not be represented as mandatory.

Look for proof in your own experience

For each important requirement, find a truthful example from work, study, volunteering, or a project. A relevant example should show context and contribution, not merely repeat the employer's phrase. If a role asks for stakeholder communication, identify the audience, the information you shared, and the purpose of the communication.

If you do not have a direct example, do not manufacture one. You can still show an adjacent capability honestly: a candidate who has used spreadsheets to reconcile data should not claim advanced SQL, but may describe the analytical work they have actually completed.

Prioritise the first page

Put the clearest, most relevant evidence where a reviewer is likely to encounter it early. That can mean adjusting the order of bullets within a recent job, moving a relevant project higher, or replacing a generic summary with a concise description of your genuine focus. Tailoring should clarify the truth; it should not rewrite your history.

Verify the listing before applying

Use the employer's official application page when possible. Confirm the role title, location, working arrangement, closing date, and application method. Be cautious if a listing asks for payment, asks for identity documents before an interview, or directs you to an unrelated messaging account. A tool score cannot prove that a listing is legitimate.
